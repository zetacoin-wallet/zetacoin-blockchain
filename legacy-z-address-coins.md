Coins on a `Z...` address belong to the retired SHA-256 chain. They are not current ZET and cannot be converted: the migration window closed on 1 August 2022 and unclaimed coins were burned to an address with no private key. An old wallet file is worth checking with a rescan, but there is no recovery route if the coins were never migrated.

## What a Z address is

Before the 2021 rebuild, Zetacoin used a `Z` prefix on a SHA-256 chain with a 160 million cap. The rebuilt chain uses Scrypt, hybrid consensus and a `9` prefix, and holders moved their coins across one for one during the migration.

A `Z...` address is therefore a record on a chain that still exists but whose coins are no longer exchangeable. Explorers will show balances; none of them can be spent into the current economy.

The full walk-through, with the checks in order, is in [What Happened to the Zetacoin Swap](https://github.com/zetacoin-wallet/zetacoin-wallet-guides/blob/main/what-happened-to-the-swap.md).

## The migration, briefly

The swap ran in stages. Old coins could be deposited to FreiExchange until 7 December 2021 and were converted automatically the next day. From December 2021 a dedicated tool swapped old coins for new at 1:1. The window closed on 1 August 2022.

When it closed, the old-chain coins that had not been migrated and the new-chain allocation projected for unclaimed coins were burned to a nulldata address with no private key. That is why the burn is final rather than administrative.

{{ARTICLE:what-happened-to-zetacoin-swap}} covers the stages in more detail.

## Checking whether your coins were migrated

An old `wallet.dat` may hold keys that correspond to a balance on the current chain, if the migration was completed for those keys. Restoring the file into a current client and letting it rescan will reveal that.

That is a check, not a promise. If the coins were never migrated, the rescan finds nothing on the current chain, because there is nothing to find.

Never send the old wallet file to anyone offering to check it. The check is something you perform locally with a copy.

For the background behind this step, [Is the Old SHA-256 Zetacoin Chain Still Running?](https://github.com/zetacoin-wallet/zetacoin-blockchain/blob/main/is-the-old-sha-256-chain-running.md) covers the same ground in more detail.

## What that balance is worth now

Nothing in the current economy. The coins exist on a retired chain, cannot be sold, cannot be staked and cannot be moved into a current wallet.

Their only remaining value is as a record: they show what was held before the migration, and in some cases they indicate whether a migration was completed for those keys.

## Why this is not a project failure

A one-for-one migration with more than a year of notice, a deposit route for exchange holders and a self-service tool for wallet holders is a reasonable process for a small coin. The failure mode was attention: holders who were not watching in mid-2022 missed it.

That is worth saying plainly because the alternative framing  -  that coins were taken  -  is wrong. Unclaimed coins were destroyed, not collected, and a burn address with no key is unspendable by anyone.

## What to do if you hold old coins

Install the current client, restore a copy of the old wallet file, and let it rescan. If a balance appears, it was migrated and is now current ZET. If not, the coins were not migrated and the matter is closed.

Then keep the old file as a record if you want the history, and back up the current wallet properly so the same situation does not repeat.

## Common questions

### Can I still convert Z coins?

No. The window closed on 1 August 2022 and the unclaimed supply was burned.

### Why does the explorer still show my old balance?

Because the old chain's history still exists. A record on a retired chain is not a convertible asset.

### Is there any recovery service?

No. The burn address has no private key, so the coins cannot be spent by anyone.

### Should I delete the old wallet file?

Keep a copy if you want the record, but do not rely on it for value, and never send it to a third party.

## Why the old balance still appears

The retired chain is still readable, so an old wallet or explorer will happily show a balance that no longer has any use. The display is accurate and the asset is not: it is a record of what was held before the migration, not a holding now.

That gap between what is visible and what is valuable is the source of most confusion about Z addresses. A number on a screen looks like money, and on a retired ledger it is a historical entry.

## The one check worth performing

Restore a copy of the old wallet file into a current client and let it rescan. If the keys were migrated, a current-chain balance appears and the old address is simply a historical detail. If nothing appears, the coins were not migrated and the matter is closed.

Do that on your own machine with a copy. No third party needs the file, and anyone asking for it is not helping.

{{ARTICLE:restore-zetacoin-wallet-backup}} covers the restore mechanics, and {{ARTICLE:old-zetacoin-chain-status}} covers the retired chain itself.

## What the old balance does and does not prove

A balance on the retired chain proves that keys controlled coins at some point. It does not prove that those coins were migrated, and it does not prove that anything remains available now.

That distinction is the whole question for anyone finding an old wallet. The rescan answers it definitively: a current balance means the migration happened, and its absence means it did not.

Either way, the useful next step is the same: protect the current wallet properly, so that a future rebuild never leaves you reconstructing a balance from a retired ledger.
